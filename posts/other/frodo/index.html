Happy Birthday, Frodo Baggins
Posted September 22, 2026 4 min read

On September 22, in the year 2968 of the Third Age the hobbit Frodo Baggins was born.

Today is Hobbit Day, the official day to commemorate the works of J.R.R. Tolkien, as per the American Tolkien Society.

To mark the occasion, I’ll relate my experience with his works throughout my life, and why I enjoy it so much.

One Ring, not rendered.

The Lord of the Rings

In 2001, Peter Jackson’s Lord of the Rings Trilogy first entered theaters with the release of The Fellowship of the Ring.

I was 10 years old then. At that point I had not yet heard of Lord of the Rings. I remember being initially uninterested in the movie, thinking it was too serious and complex. I was much more excited for Harry Potter, which was also new at the time and more child-oriented.

Eventually, I did go to see the movie with my family, and I thought it was OK. I would collect some toys, and then also play the video games with my friends. This would have been more a consequence of them being so popular, moresoe than a significant interest. None-the-less, I remember having a lot of fun playing The Two Towers and The Return of the King on Playstation 2 in co-op mode. The lore and stories did grow on me over time, and I did see the entire Trilogy during its original theatrical run.

I first read the Trilogy while in high school. It took me a month or two to get through it, and I really enjoyed it. Before reading, I would have considered myself a casual pop-culture consumer, then after finishing the trilogy I regarded myself as a real Lord of the Rings fan.

Here be no dragon.

The Hobbit

In 2012 the first Hobbit movie entered theaters, but again I wasn’t particularly interested. I was a fan of the Trilogy, but had not read any other of Tolkien’s work. I never saw these movies while they were in theaters, but I would eventually watch them at home with my family.

I share the common criticism of these movies that they are way too long, and stretched the source material farther than they should have. They felt “thin, sort of stretched, like butter scraped over too much bread.”

I don’t remember us ever actually watching the original films, instead we saw the Dustin Lee Fan Edit. It cuts the three films into one (still quite long) film. I really enjoyed the edit, and highly recommend it. Interestingly, Topher Grace (of That’s 70’s show fame) created his own edit which cut the runtime down to a mere 2 hours. I don’t believe he ever released it publically.

Just this last year I managed to read The Hobbit for the very first time, and I very much enjoyed it. Its short, which I appreciated considering my attention is not as long as it used to be.

I was shocked to see how long the book continued after the death of Smaug. It very much reminded me of the ending of Return of the King, with the battle in the Shire against Saruman and his army. Just like in ROTK I thought it was tacked on, but otherwise it didn’t bother me.
