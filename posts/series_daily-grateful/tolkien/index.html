Concerning Hobbits
Posted September 22, 2026 Updated September 23, 2026 5 min read

Today is September 22, and on this day in the year 2968 of the Third Age Frodo Baggins was born.

Today is Hobbit Day, the official day to commemorate the works of J.R.R. Tolkien as per the American Tolkien Society.

To mark the occasion, I’ve written this post to relate my experience with Tolkien’s works throughout my life, and why It matters so much to me.

One Ring, not rendered.

The Lord of the Rings

Peter Jackson’s Lord of the Rings Trilogy first entered theaters in 2001 with the release of The Fellowship of the Ring.

I was 10 years old at the time, and at that point had not yet heard of Lord of the Rings. My initial impression was disinterested: I felt that the story was too dark and complex for my taste. Like many kids in that era I was much more interested in the then-new Harry Potter series of books and films. They were much more specifically child oriented.

Eventually, I did go to see the first movie with my family, and I thought it was OK. I would collect some toys, and then also play the video games with my friends. I remember having a lot of fun playing The Two Towers and Return of the King on Playstation 2 in co-op mode. But this was certainly a consequence of how popular the movies had become, moresoe than a significant interest in the books or lore. They did grow on me over time, and I did eventually see the entire Trilogy during its original theatrical run, and by end I had fallen in love with the films.

I first read the Trilogy while in high school. It took me a month or two to get through it, and I really enjoyed it. Before reading, I would have considered myself a casual pop-culture consumer or just a fan of the films, but after finishing the books I regarded myself as a true LOTR fan.

Here be no dragon.

The Hobbit

The first of three Hobbit movies entered theaters in 2012, and again I wasn’t initially interested. I was a fan of the Trilogy, but had not read any other of Tolkien’s work. I never saw these movies while they were in theaters, but I would eventually watch them at home with my family.

The very common criticism of these movies is that they are way too long, and I agree. They stretched the source material farther than they should have. The movies felt “thin, sort of stretched, like butter scraped over too much bread.”

My memory is that we never actually watched the original three films, instead opting to watch the Dustin Lee Fan Edit instead. It cuts the three films into one (still quite long) film. I really enjoyed the edit, and highly recommend it. Interestingly, Topher Grace (of That’s 70’s show fame) created his own edit which cut the runtime down to a mere 2 hours. I don’t believe he ever released it publically.

Just a few months ago I managed to read The Hobbit for the very first time. I’ll never have nostalgia for it like I do for the Trilogy but otherwise I of course thought it was great. Its short, which I can appreciate considering my attention is not as long as it used to be.

I was shocked while reading that the book continued so long after the death of Smaug. It very much reminded me of the ending of Return of the King, with the battle in the Shire against Saruman and his army. Just like in Return of the King I felt it was tacked on, but otherwise it didn’t bother me.

The door stays shut.

The Shire

As I’ve been implying throughout this post, Lord of the Rings matters a lot to me. In large part that’s a consequence of me having been the perfect age to have had a formative experience with the films. I’m grateful for that, since outside my own opinions of the works I regard them as being a form of real mythology. A mythology intended for a specific time and place in history, the Post-War English-speaking world.

The fantasy of the Shire stuck with me the most. It will forever be pinned in my mind as being the definition of a perfect place to live. The peaceful and carefree life of a hobbit in such a place is the best life anyone could hope to have.

In case you ever wondered, “that music that plays during the hobbit scenes” is called Concerning Hobbits, and was composed by Howard Shore.
